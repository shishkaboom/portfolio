Gamification at Rev Games

SberSlovechki · word game

  • Goal: Sber has 108.5M active clients. The bank wanted more daily users in the app, more card transactions and more people spending their bonus points.
  • What: a daily word game in the loyalty program, inspired by Wordle: guess a word in six tries and win bonus points, 2,500 for a first-try guess. Only registered users play, so every game opens the bank app.
  • Research & UX: market research, an interactive prototype and a user flow that showed funnel bottlenecks. Usability tests turned onboarding into in-game learning.
  • Design: Bukvik, a bright green dictionary mascot who leads onboarding and became the face of the game in ads and social media. A playful school-like style, icons and stickers, adapted for phone, tablet and desktop and exported at 2x for Unity.
  • System: competitions, achievements and a personal dictionary so words never repeat. Design concept in two weeks, and a designer–analyst–developer workflow the studio still uses.
  • Result: 350K players in six months, 53 minutes average playtime, 21M bonus points redeemed.

RostelecomBonus Park · 3D

  • Goal: introduce subscribers to a new loyalty program, get them spending bonus points, activating discounts and checking partner offers, and bring more people to the personal account and app.
  • What: a 3D isometric theme park with a ticket office, gift shop and quest center. Each ride is a mini-game with prizes: a balloon shooter with 1,000+ levels, a Ferris wheel of fortune, Sea Treasures with a partner and a 30,000 RUB grand prize, and KHL table hockey.
  • My part: presales with marketing and pitching to the client, then art direction, user flow, usability tests, UI/UX and the design system, from 3D modelling to testing.
  • Design: AI for ride ideas and sketches, modelled in Blender. A clear, bright casual UI. Onboarding cut to three welcome pop-ups with hints, because 30% of players dropped off during onboarding in past seasons.
  • Result: about 500K players and 170K completed tasks in three months, a record for this type of gamification. The new activity helped the team hit its annual marketing plan.

MegaFonJungle Riddle · zuma shooter

  • Goal: grow daily active users in the operator’s app, sell subscriptions and partner offers, and get people playing with the brand.
  • What: a jungle arcade shooter where players fire balls into a spiral and match colours to clear chains. Built in about 1.5 months, Jul — Dec 2023.
  • My part: art direction, user flow, usability tests, UI/UX and a design system of components, icons and styles. Midjourney for the first ideas and character sketches.
  • Business mechanics: daily login bonuses, a welcome game in onboarding, a half-price retry after a loss that keeps progress, and extra attempts for completing tasks.
  • Result: average spend per player grew from 30 to 150 RUB, paid service adoption rose by 30%, and a six-month pool of marketing tasks was done in two months.

BeelineZuma City · park builder

  • What: a game inside the operator’s app, Mar 2023 — Jan 2024. Players build an amusement park: they win coins in an arcade shooter, spend them on building and upgrading rides, and collect likes that fill a progress bar with prizes.
  • My part: art direction, user flow, UX/UI, usability tests and the design system. Research on Zuma Deluxe and Frozen City, then a prototype and user flow in about three days.
  • Design: brand colours and illustrations as the base, softened with natural tones on the map. The whole isometric park is built from 2D tiles in a component system.
  • Monetization: a shop selling subscriptions for game attempts, five iterations with tests, the hardest task across all my gamification projects. Plus animated hints and specs for every interactive element.
  • Lessons: design, development and tests took about 2.5 months. Fewer than 3% of players went past three games after onboarding, so the project was closed. The takeaway: players want simple mechanics and quick wins.

Cowboys GalaxyIndie F2P · space Western

  • What: a space Western for Google Play and the App Store, made in 2020 — 2021 by an indie team building its first game. Tower defense with RPG elements; every character is an animal from the Arizona desert, led by Bill the Coati, a retired intergalactic ranger.
  • My part: art direction, character and environment design, UX/UI. I led the design team and drew the game myself: the logo, heroes, NPCs, a tile system, six planets with their own maps and backgrounds, and comic-style onboarding.
  • Problems → fixes: the shooting-gallery mechanic got repetitive, so we added character levels, harder levels, two new heroes, equipment cards and skills. Stiff sprite animation moved to Spine with a dedicated animator.
  • Monetization: two currencies, a shop, an attempt system, loot boxes and daily login rewards.
  • Launch: sprints and a design pipeline, A/B tests and corridor tests before release, store screenshots and icons for A/B tests, a gameplay video and Facebook ads.
  • What I learned: the game is on hold. Without audience research and metrics, a long tutorial lost most players, a lesson I took into the client games that followed.
